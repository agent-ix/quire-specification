## Description

Verify the shared result contract and its monotonic response to missing evidence.

## Test Procedure

Construct healthy, violating, inactive, pending, incomplete, refused, unsupported and failed results over both closed and open assessed subjects. Include an `eventually` result settled satisfied by an admitted decisive witness while the subject remains open, an `always` result settled violated by an admitted decisive counterexample while the subject remains open, and otherwise-identical open prefixes that remain pending. Exercise every valid row of the normative result-combination table, then mutate assessment execution, subject progress/closure, truth, settlement basis and each decision-support identity independently. Delete each witness or counterexample fact, trigger, role, provider observation, population member and adverse case. Mutate every provenance identity. Submit a later observation and a later closure record that each contradict one retained settlement premise; compare the old and superseding result bytes and identities. Map a result carrying simultaneous partial participation, unmet assumption and logical violation into the portable verification assessment.

## Expected Results

All valid boundary combinations admit and all one-axis forbidden combinations refuse. Decisive satisfied and violated truths retain their exact support and coexist with open subject progress; open non-decisive prefixes remain pending. Removing decision support, substituting a non-decisive basis or relabelling open progress as closed refuses the settled combination. A later contradiction creates a linked superseding or invalidating result without changing the earlier bytes, and dependent package success becomes unavailable. Result states remain structurally discriminable; missing evidence never improves truth, adequacy or package status; strict readers refuse stale identities while retaining independent valid results. The portable mapping retains all simultaneous gaps and records no evidence-sufficiency decision.
